More people are asking AI tools questions that used to go straight to Google: "Who's a good roofer in Carmel?" "What should a furnace replacement cost in Indianapolis?" Google itself now shows AI-generated summaries at the top of many searches.

So what does that mean for a local business trying to get found?

## The good news: local is still local

When someone needs a plumber *today*, they still need a real business that serves their area, with a phone number and good reviews. AI tools are pulling that information from somewhere, largely from the same sources that power traditional local search:

- Your Google Business Profile
- Your website
- Reviews across the web
- Directory listings and citations
- Mentions of your business on other sites

The fundamentals of [local SEO](/blog/local-seo-guide-indianapolis/) didn't go away. They became more important.

## What AI tools look for

AI search tools try to give confident, accurate answers. They favor businesses whose information is:

### Clear
Your website says exactly what you do, where, and for whom. No vague slogans.

### Consistent
Your name, phone number, services, and service area match everywhere. Conflicting info makes it harder for any system to trust what it finds.

### Corroborated
Other sources back you up: reviews, directory listings, local news, community sites. One website saying you're great isn't as convincing as twenty sources agreeing.

### Structured
[Schema markup](/blog/schema-markup-local-business/) labels your information in a machine-readable way, making it easier to interpret correctly.

## Practical steps for 2026

### 1. Write content that answers real questions
AI answers are built from content that directly answers questions. Pages with clear, specific answers ("A typical roof replacement in Indianapolis costs...") are more useful than vague marketing copy.

Add FAQ sections to your service and city pages. Write blog posts around the questions your customers ask on the phone.

### 2. Be specific about your service area
Mention the actual cities and neighborhoods you serve, with real context. Generic "serving the greater area" language gives AI tools nothing to work with.

### 3. Keep your Google Business Profile complete and active
Services, categories, hours, photos, posts, and Q&A. All of it is data that can be surfaced in AI answers and in the map pack.

### 4. Earn reviews that mention specifics
Reviews that mention the service and location ("They fixed our AC in Fishers the same day") are more informative than "Great job!" You can't script reviews, but you can ask customers to share what you did for them.

### 5. Get mentioned beyond your own website
Local business associations, chamber of commerce listings, sponsorships, community events, and local media all help build a footprint that confirms you're a real, established business.

### 6. Make your site technically clean
Fast loading, mobile-friendly, crawlable, with proper structure and schema. AI crawlers have the same basic needs as search crawlers.

## What not to panic about

You'll hear plenty of hype about "AI SEO" requiring a completely new strategy. For local service businesses, that's mostly not true. The businesses doing well in AI-driven results are generally the same ones doing well in traditional local search: clear websites, strong profiles, consistent info, and real reviews.

## The bottom line

Search is evolving, but the businesses that win are still the ones that are easy to understand, easy to verify, and easy to trust.
