Every SEO strategy starts with one question: **what are people actually typing into Google?**

Business owners often guess wrong. You might call it "HVAC system replacement." Your customers search "new furnace cost" or "AC not blowing cold air." Keyword research closes that gap.

## The three types of local searches

### 1. Service + location
"Roof repair Fishers," "dentist in Greenwood," "web design Indianapolis." These are high-intent searches from people ready to hire. They're your top priority.

### 2. "Near me" searches
"Plumber near me," "auto repair near me." Google uses the searcher's location to decide what to show. You win these mostly through your [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-checklist/) and proximity.

### 3. Problem and question searches
"Why is my basement flooding," "how much does a new roof cost in Indiana." These are earlier in the buying journey and are perfect topics for blog posts.

## Step 1: Brainstorm like a customer

Start with a list of every service you offer. Then, for each one, write down:

- What would a customer call it? (Not the industry term.)
- What problem does it solve?
- What would they search when that problem happens?

Ask your front desk or whoever answers the phone. They hear how customers describe their problems every day.

## Step 2: Use Google itself (free)

Google gives you keyword ideas for free:

- **Autocomplete:** Start typing your service and see what Google suggests.
- **People Also Ask:** The question boxes in search results are real customer questions.
- **Related searches:** Scroll to the bottom of the results page.
- **The map pack:** Look at the categories and services your top competitors list.

## Step 3: Check your own data

If you have a website, **Google Search Console** shows the actual searches people used to find you, including ones where you showed up but didn't get clicked. This is gold. It often reveals keywords you're close to ranking for.

Your Google Business Profile performance report also shows search terms.

## Step 4: Look at competitors

Search your main service and city. Look at the businesses ranking at the top:

- What pages do they have?
- How do they name their services?
- What cities do they target?

You're not copying them. You're finding the gaps.

## Step 5: Prioritize

Not every keyword is worth chasing. Prioritize by:

1. **Intent:** Will this person hire someone? "Emergency plumber Carmel" beats "how do pipes work."
2. **Value:** Does it lead to your most profitable services?
3. **Competition:** Can you realistically rank? Suburban terms are often easier than citywide Indianapolis terms.

## Step 6: Map keywords to pages

This is where it comes together. Each important keyword gets a home:

| Keyword type | Page |
|---|---|
| Main service + city | Service page |
| Service + suburb | [City page](/blog/city-pages-without-duplicate-content/) |
| Questions and problems | Blog posts |
| Brand searches | Homepage |

One page per main topic. Don't make several pages compete for the same keyword.

## Common mistakes

- **Targeting only one big keyword.** "Plumber Indianapolis" is great, but dozens of smaller searches add up to more traffic.
- **Using industry jargon** your customers don't use.
- **Ignoring suburbs.** Carmel, Fishers, Greenwood, and Avon all have their own search demand.
- **Chasing volume over intent.** A keyword with 50 searches a month from buyers beats 5,000 from people just curious.
