Reviews do two jobs at once. They help you rank in the Google map pack, and they convince people to call you instead of the business next to you.

Most happy customers are willing to leave a review. They just don't think to do it unless you ask, and make it easy.

## Rule #1: Just ask

The biggest reason businesses don't have reviews is that they never ask. Make it part of your process, not an afterthought.

The best time to ask is **right after a great experience**: when the job is done, the customer is happy, and you're still fresh in their mind.

## Rule #2: Make it ridiculously easy

Don't say "find us on Google and leave a review." That's homework.

Instead:

1. Go to your Google Business Profile dashboard
2. Click **"Ask for reviews"** to get your direct review link
3. Send that link directly. One tap opens the review box.

## Rule #3: Text beats email

Texts get opened. A short, personal text right after the job works well:

> "Hi Sarah, it's Mike from Smith Plumbing. Thanks again for having us out today! If you have 30 seconds, a Google review would really help our small business: [link]. Thank you!"

Short, personal, and specific.

## Other places to ask

- **On your invoice or receipt.** Add a QR code that links to your review page.
- **In a follow-up email** a day or two after service.
- **Leave-behind cards** for service visits.
- **In person.** "If you were happy with everything, a Google review would mean a lot to us."

## What NOT to do

These can get reviews removed, or get your profile penalized:

- **Don't offer incentives.** No discounts, gift cards, or entries into drawings in exchange for reviews. It's against Google's policy.
- **Don't buy reviews.** Ever. Google is aggressive about catching fake reviews.
- **Don't "review gate."** That means asking customers whether they're happy first and only sending happy ones to Google. Google prohibits it.
- **Don't write reviews for yourself** or have employees do it.
- **Don't ask for all your reviews at once.** A steady flow looks more natural and helps more over time.

## How to respond to reviews

**Respond to every review.** It shows customers you care, and it's a chance to show off your customer service publicly.

### Positive reviews
Keep it short and personal. Mention the service naturally if it fits:

> "Thanks so much, Sarah! Glad we could get your water heater replaced the same day. Enjoy the hot showers!"

### Negative reviews
This is where you can really stand out:

1. **Don't respond angry.** Wait until you're calm.
2. **Thank them** for the feedback.
3. **Acknowledge** the issue without arguing details publicly.
4. **Take it offline:** "Please call me directly at..."
5. **Keep it short.**

Future customers read your response more than the complaint. A calm, professional reply can actually build trust.

## How many reviews do you need?

There's no magic number. What matters is how you compare to the businesses you're competing with in the map pack. Look at the top three results for your main keyword in your city. That's your benchmark.

Consistency matters more than a big one-time push. A few new reviews every month is a strong, healthy signal.
