Most small business websites are built, launched, and then... forgotten. The person who built it moves on. Nobody remembers the login. The site sits there for years.

It might look fine from the outside. But under the hood, problems are piling up.

## 1. Your contact form quietly breaks

This is the scariest one. Forms break for all kinds of reasons: an expired API key, a plugin update, an email setting change. The form still *looks* fine, and visitors still fill it out. But the messages go nowhere.

You never know how many leads you lost.

**Prevention:** Test your own contact form at least once a month. Better yet, have someone monitoring it.

## 2. Security vulnerabilities pile up

Sites built on platforms like WordPress depend on plugins and themes that need regular updates. Outdated plugins are one of the most common ways small business sites get hacked.

A hacked site might redirect visitors to spam, display unwanted ads, or get flagged by Google with a warning that scares visitors away.

## 3. Information goes stale

Old hours. Services you no longer offer. A team member who left two years ago. A "2023 Special" banner. Customers notice, and it makes them wonder if you're still in business.

## 4. Rankings slip

Google favors sites that stay fresh and work well. Over time, an unmaintained site falls behind:

- Competitors publish new content and update their pages
- Broken links accumulate
- Site speed degrades as plugins pile up
- Technical standards evolve and your site doesn't

## 5. The SSL certificate expires

When your security certificate lapses, browsers show visitors a big "Not Secure" warning. Most people leave immediately.

## 6. Hosting surprises

Hosting renewal prices jump, or the host shuts down an old server, and suddenly your site is offline. If nobody's watching, it could be down for days before anyone notices.

## What good website maintenance includes

A solid maintenance plan should cover:

- **Hosting** on fast, reliable infrastructure
- **SSL** that renews automatically
- **Security** monitoring and updates
- **Backups**, so mistakes can be undone
- **Uptime monitoring** to catch outages fast
- **Content edits** for hours, services, prices, and photos
- **Form testing** to make sure leads arrive
