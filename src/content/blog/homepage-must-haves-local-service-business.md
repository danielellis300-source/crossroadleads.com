Your homepage gets more visitors than any other page on your site. It has seconds to answer three questions: *What do you do? Where? Why should I call you?*

Here are the ten elements I make sure every local service business homepage has.

## 1. A headline that says what you do and where

Not "Welcome!" and not "Excellence in Every Detail." Be specific:

- "Roof Repair & Replacement in Indianapolis and Hamilton County"
- "Family Dentistry in Greenwood, Indiana"

A visitor should understand your business from the headline alone.

## 2. A tap-to-call phone number in the header

Visible on every page, clickable on mobile. This is non-negotiable for service businesses. Bonus: a sticky call button that follows visitors as they scroll on mobile.

## 3. One clear primary call to action

"Get a Free Estimate." "Book an Appointment." "Call Now." Pick one main action and make it obvious. Repeat it down the page.

## 4. Trust signals above the fold

Give visitors reasons to believe you right away:

- Star rating and review count
- Years in business
- Licensed and insured
- Guarantees
- Certifications or affiliations

## 5. A clear list of services

Show your main services with short descriptions, each linking to its own dedicated page. This helps visitors and gives Google a clear map of what you do.

## 6. Your service area

List the cities you serve, ideally linking to [individual city pages](/blog/city-pages-without-duplicate-content/). Someone in Avon wants to know you'll come to Avon.

## 7. Real photos

Your team, your trucks, your work. Real photos build trust in a way stock photos never will. A smiling stock-photo technician is instantly recognizable, and it makes visitors wonder what else isn't real.

## 8. Social proof

Reviews and testimonials from real customers, ideally with first names and the city. If you're a newer business without many reviews yet, lean on guarantees, your process, and your story instead. Never fake it.

## 9. A simple explanation of how it works

A short 3–4 step process ("Call → Free estimate → We do the work → You're happy") reduces uncertainty. People are more likely to take the first step when they know what comes next.

## 10. A short contact form

Name, phone, email, and what they need. Some people prefer not to call, especially after hours. Give them an easy way to reach you, and make sure it actually works. (See [what happens when nobody maintains your website](/blog/website-maintenance-why-it-matters/).)

## Bonus: speed

None of this matters if the page takes forever to load. Aim for under 2–3 seconds on mobile. More in [why website speed matters](/blog/website-speed-core-web-vitals/).

## Score your homepage

Open your homepage on your phone and count how many of these ten you have.

- **9–10:** You're in great shape. Focus on SEO and traffic.
- **6–8:** A few tweaks could noticeably increase calls.
- **Under 6:** Your homepage is probably costing you business.
