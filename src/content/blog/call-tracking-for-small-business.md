Ask most local business owners where their customers come from and you'll hear: "Mostly word of mouth... some from Google, I think?"

*I think* is expensive. If you don't know which marketing produces calls, you can't do more of what works or stop paying for what doesn't. Call tracking fixes that.

## What is call tracking?

Call tracking uses unique phone numbers for different marketing sources. Each number forwards to your regular phone, so nothing changes for you or your customers. But behind the scenes, every call is logged with where it came from.

For example:

- **Website number:** shows on your website
- **Google Business Profile number:** used on your GBP
- **Ads number:** used in Google Ads or Facebook ads
- **Print number:** on your truck wrap, yard signs, or mailers

When a call comes in, you know exactly which source produced it.

## Dynamic number insertion

More advanced setups use **dynamic number insertion**: your website automatically shows a different number depending on how the visitor arrived. Someone from Google organic sees one number; someone from a Facebook ad sees another.

This can even tell you which **page** or **keyword** led to a call.

## "Won't this mess up my local SEO?"

It's a valid concern. Your business name, address, and phone number (NAP) should be consistent across the web, and random numbers everywhere can confuse Google.

Done correctly, call tracking doesn't hurt your SEO:

- **On your Google Business Profile**, use the tracking number as the primary number and list your main number as an **additional phone**. Google supports this setup.
- **On your website**, dynamic insertion swaps the number visually for visitors while your real number stays in the code and schema markup that Google reads.
- **On directories and citations**, keep your main number. Don't use tracking numbers there.

## What you learn from call tracking

Once it's running, you'll know:

- **How many calls** each marketing source produces
- **What time of day** calls come in (are you missing calls after 5pm?)
- **Missed calls** you need to return
- **Cost per lead** for each channel
- **Call quality**, if you enable recording (with proper disclosure)

This is the kind of data that changes decisions. Maybe your Google Business Profile produces more calls than your website. Maybe that expensive directory listing produces nothing.

## Who needs call tracking?

Call tracking makes the most sense for businesses that:

- Get most of their leads by phone (contractors, home services, medical, legal, auto)
- Spend money on more than one marketing channel
- Want to measure the return on their SEO or ads

If phone calls are how you make money, you should know where they come from.
