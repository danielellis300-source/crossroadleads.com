"How much does a website cost?" is the first question almost every business owner asks me, and the honest answer is: it depends on what you need the website to *do*.

A website that just needs to exist is cheap. A website that needs to rank on Google and make your phone ring is a different product. Here's a straight breakdown of what Indianapolis businesses typically pay in 2026, and what you get at each level.

## The four ways to get a website

### 1. DIY website builders ($15–$50/month)

Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy, and similar platforms let you drag and drop your own site. It's the cheapest option in dollars, but not in time.

**Good for:** hobby businesses, brand-new side hustles, anyone who just needs a basic online presence.

**The catch:** you're the designer, copywriter, and SEO. Most DIY sites end up slow, generic, and invisible on Google, not because the platforms are bad, but because ranking takes more than a nice template.

### 2. Freelancers ($500–$3,000 one-time)

A freelancer builds the site, hands you the keys, and moves on to the next project.

**Good for:** businesses that just need a professional-looking site and plan to handle marketing themselves.

**The catch:** what happens six months later when you need an update, the site breaks, or you realize nobody can find it? Many freelancers don't offer ongoing support or SEO.

### 3. Local web design + SEO partners ($1,000–$5,000 + monthly)

This is where Crossroad Leads sits. You get a custom site built for speed and search, plus ongoing hosting, edits, and optionally local SEO.

**Good for:** service businesses that want leads, not just a pretty page.

### 4. Full-service agencies ($5,000–$25,000+)

Big agencies bring big teams: strategists, designers, developers, and account managers.

**Good for:** larger companies with complex needs and marketing budgets to match.

**The catch:** small businesses often pay for overhead and get passed to a junior team member.

## What actually drives the price

Whoever you hire, these are the factors that move the number up or down:

- **Number of pages.** A 5-page site and a 30-page site with city and service pages are very different projects.
- **Copywriting.** Writing good, search-optimized content takes real time. Some providers include it; many don't.
- **Custom design vs. template.** Templates are faster. Custom design fits your brand and conversion goals.
- **Functionality.** Booking systems, e-commerce, client portals, and integrations add cost.
- **SEO setup.** Proper schema markup, metadata, site structure, and speed optimization are often skipped on cheap builds.
- **Ongoing care.** Hosting, security, backups, and updates are either included in a monthly plan or left for you to figure out.

## The real question: cost vs. return

Here's how I'd think about it. If your average customer is worth $500 to your business, and a well-built site brings in just two extra customers a month, that's $1,000 in monthly revenue from a website that might cost $99–$299 a month to maintain.

A cheap website that never ranks isn't a bargain. It's a cost with no return.
