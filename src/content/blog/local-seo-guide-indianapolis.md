When someone in Indianapolis searches "plumber near me" or "best tacos in Fountain Square," Google shows them a very specific set of results. Local SEO is the work of making sure *your* business is in those results.

This guide covers the fundamentals in plain English, with no jargon.

## What makes local SEO different

Regular SEO is about ranking for a topic anywhere. Local SEO is about ranking for a topic **in a place**. Google treats these searches differently because the searcher wants something nearby, usually right now.

For local searches, Google typically shows:

1. **The map pack.** The map with three business listings near the top of the page.
2. **Organic results.** The traditional blue links below it.

Showing up in both is the goal. The map pack gets a huge share of clicks for local searches, so it's often the first priority.

## The three things Google looks at

Google has said publicly that local rankings come down to three factors:

- **Relevance:** How well does your business match what the person searched for?
- **Distance:** How close is your business to the searcher (or the location in their search)?
- **Prominence:** How well-known and trusted is your business online? Think reviews, links, mentions, and overall web presence.

You can't change your distance. But you can absolutely improve relevance and prominence.

## Step 1: Claim and optimize your Google Business Profile

Your [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-checklist/) (GBP) is the single most important local SEO asset you have. It controls how you appear in the map pack and on Google Maps.

At minimum:

- Choose the most accurate **primary category** (this matters a lot)
- Add every service you offer
- Set your service area to the Indy-area cities you actually serve
- Upload real photos of your work, team, and vehicles
- Keep your hours accurate, including holidays

## Step 2: Build a website Google can understand

Your website backs up everything your GBP claims. Google wants to see:

- **A dedicated page for each core service.** Not one page that lists everything.
- **Pages for the areas you serve.** If you work in Carmel, Fishers, and Greenwood, you need content about those places. (Here's [how to do city pages the right way](/blog/city-pages-without-duplicate-content/).)
- **Consistent contact info.** Your business name and phone number should match your GBP exactly.
- **Speed.** Slow sites lose visitors and rankings. More on that in my post on [website speed](/blog/website-speed-core-web-vitals/).
- **Schema markup.** Code that tells Google exactly what your business is and where it operates. See [schema markup explained](/blog/schema-markup-local-business/).

## Step 3: Get reviews, consistently

Reviews feed directly into prominence. They also convert. People compare star ratings and review counts before they ever click.

The key word is *consistently*. Ten reviews in one week followed by nothing for a year looks odd. A steady flow of new reviews is healthier. I cover practical ways to do this in [how to get more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews/).

## Step 4: Get listed in the right places

Citations are mentions of your business name, address, and phone number on other websites: directories like Yelp, Bing Places, Apple Maps, the BBB, and industry-specific sites.

Accuracy matters more than volume. One wrong phone number on an old directory can confuse Google about which info is correct.

## Step 5: Create helpful local content

Blog posts and resources that answer real customer questions build topical authority. "How much does a roof replacement cost in Indianapolis?" is a far better post than "5 Roofing Tips."

## How long does it take?

Usually 3–6 months to see meaningful results, sometimes faster in suburbs like Zionsville or Greenfield, and sometimes slower for competitive Indianapolis keywords. I break that down in [how long SEO takes](/blog/how-long-does-seo-take/).
