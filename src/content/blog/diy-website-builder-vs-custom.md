I build custom websites for a living, so you might expect me to say DIY builders are terrible. They're not. For some businesses, they're the right call.

Here's an honest comparison so you can decide for yourself.

## When a DIY builder makes sense

Wix, Squarespace, and similar tools are genuinely good products. They make sense if:

- You're just starting out and money is tight
- You mainly need a place to send people who already know about you
- You enjoy design and have time to learn
- Your business doesn't depend on Google for new customers

If that's you, go for it. Pick a clean template, write clear copy, and put your phone number everywhere.

## Where DIY builders fall short

The trouble starts when you need your website to **bring in new customers from Google**.

### Speed
Builders load a lot of code to power their drag-and-drop features, whether your page uses them or not. Most DIY sites score poorly on mobile PageSpeed, and [speed matters](/blog/website-speed-core-web-vitals/).

### SEO limitations
You can edit titles and descriptions, but deeper control over structure, schema markup, and technical details is limited or awkward.

### Generic design
Thousands of businesses use the same templates. Customers notice, especially in polished markets like Carmel or Zionsville.

### Your time
This is the hidden cost. The hours you spend dragging boxes around, writing copy, and figuring out SEO are hours you're not spending on your business.

### You're renting, not owning
Your site lives on their platform. If you leave, you usually can't take the design with you. If they raise prices, you pay.

## The true cost comparison

| | DIY Builder | Custom (Crossroad Leads Starter) |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | $0 | $997 |
| Monthly cost | ~$20–$50 | $99 |
| Your time | 20–60+ hours | ~2 hours (interview + review) |
| Copywriting | You | Included |
| Speed | Usually average | 90+ PageSpeed |
| SEO setup | Basic | Full on-page + schema |
| Edits | You | Included |
| Ownership | Platform-locked | You own it |

When you factor in your own time, the gap is a lot smaller than it looks.

## The middle path

If you're on a DIY builder now and it's working okay, you don't have to switch immediately. A few things that help:

1. Make sure your phone number is a tap-to-call link in the header
2. Create a separate page for each service you offer
3. Compress your images before uploading
4. Fill out your [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-checklist/) completely

When you're ready to grow and want Google to send you customers consistently, that's the time to invest in a custom site.

## My honest take

If your website is a digital business card, DIY is fine. If it's supposed to be a lead generation machine, build it like one.
